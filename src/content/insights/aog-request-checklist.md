An urgent parts enquiry becomes more useful when the first message gives the supplier something specific to act on. A short, organised request helps separate the sourcing question from the technical and logistical questions that still need an answer. It cannot guarantee a recovery time, but it can reduce avoidable clarification.

Treat the first AOG message as a handover between teams. Someone receiving it should be able to identify the required part, understand the aircraft context, reach a responsible contact, and see the next delivery constraint without searching through several earlier emails.

## Lead with the part and aircraft

Put the requested part number near the beginning of the message. Include any suffix and the quantity. State the aircraft type and, where relevant to the requirement, the engine or system involved. If you have an alternate part number, identify it as an alternate and explain whether your technical team has accepted it.

Avoid writing only a component description such as “valve needed urgently.” A description is useful context but may refer to multiple variants. If you do not yet have the full identification, say what is missing and who can confirm it. An unresolved technical question should remain visible rather than being concealed by an urgent subject line.

## Separate acceptable condition from required paperwork

State which offered conditions your organisation can evaluate. Then list the mandatory documents separately: for example, a particular release, trace to source, or a non-incident statement where relevant. Mention any configuration or history restriction that would make an otherwise available offer unusable.

Do not assume that a fast shipment can compensate for a missing document your receiving process requires. Ask for a legible review package early. The source’s release and trace information must still correspond to the actual offered item; urgency does not make a similar unit interchangeable.

## Give a usable delivery point

Identify the country and exact delivery arrangements to be agreed with the supplier. An airport code alone may not identify the consignee, receiving facility, or customs route. Provide the receiving contact, opening or handover constraints, and any handling limitations relevant to the part.

Describe the required arrival time with a date and timezone. Distinguish “needed for a scheduled maintenance window” from “aircraft currently grounded.” Avoid ambiguous phrases such as “tomorrow morning” when several countries and shifts are involved. If your receiving plan changes, send a clearly labelled update to everyone coordinating the movement.

## Put the decision-makers in the loop

Include a callback number and an email address monitored during the event. Identify who can approve the commercial offer and who can review technical acceptability. If the person requesting the quote cannot approve an alternative or an urgent freight charge, say who can.

A practical handover also says when the contact changes. Supply a replacement name or mailbox before a shift ends. Repeating a callback attempt to an unmonitored number is an avoidable delay, even when the supplier has a useful option ready to discuss.

## Define the commercial boundaries

Explain whether the requirement is an outright purchase or another specifically agreed arrangement. Where a budget, purchase-order process, or internal approval is essential, bring that into the conversation early. The supplier should be able to distinguish a sourcing enquiry from authority to commit to a purchase or transport booking.

End use and destination information can be needed for trade-control review. Prepare accurate details and respond promptly to follow-up questions. Urgency is not a substitute for any required authorisation or screening.

## A compact first message

Use a clear subject: “AOG — part number — aircraft type — required location.” In the body, list quantity, acceptable condition, mandatory documents, destination, required arrival time and timezone, and a reachable callback contact. Attach a concise part list if there are multiple items; mark the items that drive the event.
