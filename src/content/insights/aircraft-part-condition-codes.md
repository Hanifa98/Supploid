Condition codes make a parts list easier to scan. They do not replace a description of the offered item, its maintenance history, or the documents available from its source. When two quotations carry the same short code, the underlying offers can still differ in ways that matter to purchasing and technical acceptance.

The expansions below describe common trading usage. Supplier conventions can differ, so ask the seller to define the condition in the actual quotation. None of these letters, alone, demonstrates installation eligibility, remaining life, a warranty, or the availability of a particular release document.

## New-condition shorthand

**FN — Factory New.** Commonly used for an item represented as factory new. Confirm the source, date-related information relevant to the part, and available manufacturer documentation. Do not infer the exact warranty or sales channel from the abbreviation.

**NE — New.** Generally describes a new, unused item. Ask what evidence supports that description and whether storage history, packaging, shelf life, or configuration needs review. A new-condition label does not answer every traceability question.

**NS — New Surplus.** Commonly describes unused material available through surplus supply. Establish what records accompany it and whether any storage or age limits apply to your requirement. Surplus is a commercial description; it does not itself establish how a particular item was preserved.

These distinctions are useful for enquiry routing, but your RFQ should describe the limits that matter. If your organisation requires a specific source relationship or document chain, state that explicitly rather than expecting FN or NE to carry the entire requirement.

## Maintenance-related shorthand

**OH — Overhauled.** Describes an offered overhaul condition. Ask for the relevant release and work records, the work standard used, and any time or cycle information needed for review. An overhaul claim should not be converted into an assumption that every life counter resets or the item becomes equivalent to newly manufactured material.

**SV — Serviceable.** Common trading language indicating that the item is offered in serviceable condition. Ask what work and documentation support that offer. Do not assume SV means a recent overhaul, a specific remaining life, or suitability for your intended aircraft configuration.

**RP — Repaired.** Indicates that repair work forms part of the item’s declared status. The buyer should establish the scope of that work from the available records. A repair description should not be silently treated as an overhaul.

**IN — Inspected.** Commonly used for an inspection-related condition, sometimes paired with testing in a supplier’s description. Ask what was inspected or tested and what statement the originator actually makes. The short code does not communicate the complete work scope.

## As removed needs a defined plan

**AR — As Removed.** Describes an item offered in its removed condition. It should not be treated as ready for installation merely because the donor aircraft was operating, or because a listing has a photograph and a serial number. Clarify removal history, available records, and the evaluation or maintenance route your organisation plans to use.

If you are buying an AR item as a core or for assessment, make that purchasing purpose clear. Compare the total commercial proposal, including the work and uncertainty that remain, rather than comparing its price directly with an apparently ready-to-use offer.

## Separate the code from the document package

Specify mandatory paperwork independently of condition. A request might accept several conditions while requiring particular release and trace documents for each offered item. A seller then has a clear basis for saying which options meet the requirement and which do not.

The [FAA’s Form 8130-3 guidance](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/other_visit/aviation_industry/designees_delegations/resources/8130-3QA.pdf) explains why even a release document is not, by itself, installation eligibility. The procurement implication is to keep condition, accompanying records, and the installer’s technical decision as separate review questions.

## Make your acceptable range explicit

In your inquiry message, list all conditions your team can consider, then add the constraints behind that selection: required release, trace, configuration, shelf life, or other relevant history. Explain whether alternatives need a separate approval and whether the offered unit must be reviewed before order acceptance.
